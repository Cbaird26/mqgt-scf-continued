# Assumptions and limits

1. **Inherited action.** The parity-even full-Dirac spectral action is `(kappa/2) Tr f(D6^2/Lambda^2)`, with the positive ten-exponential common-vacuum profiles copied byte for byte from the pinned predecessor. Four repaired Weyl blocks are counted with the predecessor's full-Dirac multiplicities. No measured coupling is an input.
2. **Microscopic insertion.** The two lowest scalar monopole harmonics enter the Hermitian operator as `Gamma7 M[Phi]`, with exactly the predecessor's n=2 to n=1 Yukawa links. The inferred opposite six-dimensional chiralities remain an inherited assumption. This calculation evaluates that specified operator; it does not establish its uniqueness or a UV origin.
3. **Geometric weak lift.** The torsion-free metric lift and rotation-equivariant monopole connection are those in the supplied Geometric Weak-Gauge Response note. The curvature/Pauli vertex is retained with its fixed coefficient. It is not replaced by an independently gauged representation-only SU(2).
4. **Fields and amplitudes.** `Phi_i = (H_i/r) Y_(1/2)` and `h_i = H_i/(Lambda R)`; hence `M/Lambda = yhat_i h_i Y`. The displayed neutral matrices use four copied blocks and `yhat_d=yhat_e=yhat_u=1`. Their entries multiply h_d^2 and h_u^2, not canonically normalized vevs. The note gives the conversion.
5. **Scope of exactness.** Internal fermion modes are retained in convergent spectral representations, without a local expansion in 1/R. Numerically these are finite angular cutoffs. The scalar potential is expanded through fourth order in the lowest-harmonic fields, its kinetic term at the symmetric background through two external derivatives, and the neutral kinetic shifts through second order in the vevs. A small finite-background response is also evaluated directly. No entire all-field effective action is claimed.
6. **Operational kinetic definition.** K is the coefficient of q^2 in the transverse neutral gauge two-point function at a prescribed constant Higgs background. This includes the seagull, commutator, orbital, and spin-connection contributions. It is not an isolated Wilson coefficient of only `(H^dagger H) F^2`, which requires an operator-basis convention and field redefinitions.
7. **Vacuum and mass convention.** The inherited radius is fixed at R_star. The two profiles are compared at identical noncanonical h_i. Their scalar normalization now differs. No new coupled scalar/radius/axion vacuum is solved, and neither the illustrative finite background nor a quartic minimum is asserted to be the physical vacuum. The mass coefficient is untuned; no new hierarchy counterterm is introduced. `m_H^2=2 A2/G` is the quadratic mass parameter in the low-momentum derivative expansion, not a computed physical pole mass. Its order-Lambda magnitude precludes using the truncated momentum expansion to locate a pole.
8. **Other fields.** Higher scalar harmonics, bosonic fluctuations, the radius kinetic term and the anomaly-cancelling sector have not been integrated out. The pure-X Stueckelberg assumption preserves the photon null direction but its mass, six-dimensional anomaly polynomial, and kinetic/threshold effects are still not derived.
9. **Quantum scope.** These are classical spectral-action coefficients. The proper-time-like heat kernels are not a quantum loop computation. Independent kappa selection, the physical spectrum, renormalization conditions, thresholds, and running are still required. The measured fine-structure constant remains unexplained.
10. **Numerics and review.** Source inputs retain 95 decimal digits; new calculations use float64/complex128. Cutoff, contour, step-size, sphere-quadrature, and independent external-Clifford checks estimate numerical stability. No interval rounding enclosure, universal tail certificate, or external peer review is claimed.

The geometric weak field enters the six-dimensional scalar curvature. The a4 term proportional to `R6 tr(M^2)` therefore contributes to `h^2 F_W^2`; the geometric WW shift does not first arise at a6.
