1. Executed data analysis
------------------------
Dataset: Sebastian Pedalino, Bruno Eduardo Ramirez Galindo, Richard Ferstl,
Klaus Hornberger, Markus Arndt and Stefan Gerlich, Probing quantum mechanics
with nanoparticle matter-wave interferometry - Dataset and code,
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17502163 .
Associated paper: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09917-9 .
Original zip MD5: 7917961b27676f4e9df3af6d3b01c374 (matches record).
Original zip SHA256:
b92355d06e06f16b3c746020ef7bc6c6eac6a629d36d739aff79e6a894003b9a.
The original files are preserved. The reanalysis code is new and separate.

All 95 April 22 power scans are read from the accompanying spreadsheet and
raw .dat files, with 41 recorded positions per scan (3895 bins total). They
contain 10,772,714 recorded counts. This is real public experimental data,
not an experiment performed in this continuation.

For each scan, fit the working model
  mu_k = a [1 + b cos(2 pi x_k/d) + c sin(2 pi x_k/d)],
with d=133 nm, a>0 and sqrt(b^2+c^2)<1. The fit minimizes the independent-
Poisson negative log likelihood using its analytic gradient and Hessian.
The cosine/sine representation has no artificial phase boundary. No modulo-
period binning is needed for this count-level fit. The reported visibility is
sqrt(b^2+c^2). Counts are taken exactly as recorded; the working model does
not establish that detector output events are independent Poisson arrivals.

Results: pooled Pearson statistic / 3610 residual degrees of freedom is
3.43937761948. The per-scan median is 3.36831933497; 92 of 95 scans exceed 2.
The pooled Poisson deviance is 12413.3810805. Thus naive Poisson Fisher errors
cannot be treated as calibrated exclusion uncertainties. Overdispersion here
means residual variation relative to this working model, not a diagnosed
microscopic noise mechanism or a new-field signal.

Independent ordinary least-squares sinusoidal visibilities differ by at most
0.000721960459 (0.072196 percentage points); their median absolute difference
is 0.0000501143641. The fitted central visibility is comparatively insensitive
to this likelihood change; statistical coverage is a different question.

Adding a free second harmonic gives pooled Pearson/df=3.44497884925. Adding
both that harmonic and a linear scan-order trend gives 3.40112041866. These
diagnostics reduce raw deviance but do not remove the residual problem after
accounting for their extra parameters. They do not rule out other instrument
models, detector statistics, time correlations, or additional harmonics.

2. Exact contrast/attenuation identifiability obstruction
-------------------------------------------------------
If a proposed attenuation is common to every power setting,
  V_obs(P) = A V_Q(P) exp(-chi),
then log A -> log A + delta and chi -> chi + delta leave every expected count
unchanged. The derivatives with respect to log A and chi have opposite signs,
so their two-column Fisher block has rank one for nonzero contrast. Data can
identify B=A exp(-chi) but cannot uniquely separate A and chi without further
information. This remains true for a common A with a fixed quantum curve;
allowing a separate contrast nuisance for each scan only weakens identification.

The delivered ridge CSV uses each scan's fitted effective contrast and chi
between 0 and 0.4. Each rescaled contrast stays below one. Expected counts
change by at most 9.1e-13 and total deviance by at most 3.9e-11 (roundoff).
The ridge is a reparameterization of the fitted data, not a fitted quantum
baseline or an empirical upper bound on chi. Physical A<=1 can truncate an
otherwise calibrated ridge; it does not identify its point or a mechanism.

The obstruction does not apply to every possible scalar signal. A predicted
dependence on velocity, path separation, material, mass, or controlled bath
state that is linearly independent of calibrated nuisances can be identifiable.
That dependence must follow from the matched theory and measured state.

3. Apparatus values recovered from the author code
------------------------------------------------
Grating period: 133 nm. Grating gaps: L1=L2=0.98375 m. Beam mean longitudinal
velocity: 158 m/s; Gaussian standard deviation: 9 m/s. Grating beam waists:
560, 540 and 575 micrometers. The source analysis assumes unit reflectivity
and ionization probability. These are author-code inputs, not newly calibrated
measurements. The mass spectrum is interpreted as doubly charged and selected
within 0.85--1.15 of nominal 170,000 Da; the original analysis gives about
168.94 kDa mean mass. Power, position, exposure and count fields come from
the actual scan metadata. Original analysis code is included for provenance.

At that mean mass and speed, total flight is 12.4525 ms and the symmetric
first-harmonic triangular-path estimate reaches about 110.57 nm. Neither the
full flight at a constant maximum separation nor a single geometric path is
the complete Talbot-Lau observable. Average complex harmonic coefficients over
mass and velocity before forming visibility. Do not average visibility ratios.

4. Theory-to-counts interface that remains to be completed
--------------------------------------------------------
The common symmetric portal has V_portal=(phi^T K phi) HdaggerH/2. At low
transfer, elementary fermion/nucleon matching gives C=f_a m_a K/(2 m_h^2)
in B=s^T C s. A composite nanoparticle requires a matched density response,
its spatial form factor, recoil kinematics, and a decision about coherent
versus incoherent scattering. Multiplying a nucleon coefficient by a mass
number without checking momentum transfer is not sufficient.

For a specified stationary bath, define the connected symmetrized spectrum
of B. In natural units, the leading weak-coupling second-cumulant attenuation
for a specified pair of paths has the schematic filter form
  chi = (1/2) integral dOmega/(2pi) d^3Q/(2pi)^3
                S_B_sym(Omega,Q) |F(Q,Omega)|^2,
  F = integral dt exp(-i Omega t)
       [exp(i Q.x_L(t))-exp(i Q.x_R(t))] F_object(Q).
This formula declares a leading approximation; the quadratic bath operator
is not exactly Gaussian. Switching, composite response and ultraviolet
renormalization must be specified before finite-time integration is justified.
The two-field case contracts the connected O_ij=s_i s_j spectrum with
C_ij C_kl, not a freely fitted rate constant.

For stationary heavy point paths and a controlled Markov limit the source
archive gives gamma(d)=S_B(0,0)-S_B(0,d). A moving probe samples Doppler-shifted
frequencies and generally needs S_B(Omega,Q), not just its zero-frequency
slice. The approximation of a stationary probe requires small velocity relative
to relevant bath velocities, not merely v much smaller than light speed.

A Talbot-Lau prediction needs the appropriate harmonic/path filters R_l,
complex grating coefficients S_l, joint mass/velocity distribution W(m,v),
detector response, background, exposure, and calibrated instrument nuisances:
  mu_jk = exposure_j * [background_jk + efficiency_j *
         integral dm dv W(m,v) sum_l S_l(P_j;m,v) R_l(m,v)
                            exp(2 pi i l x_jk/d)].
The sum must yield real nonnegative expected counts; conjugate harmonics,
finite geometry and the detector response enforce that condition.

The bath population is a physical input. A cosmological ideal-bath checkpoint
at T~GeV is not a measured laboratory scalar bath. The matching field equation
is multiplicative, (Box+M^2)s=2 n_a C s+...; ordinary matter does not thereby
become a calibrated additive scalar source. Zero field remains a solution.

5. Concrete completion criteria
-------------------------------
A. Fix and independently calibrate the composite coupling/form factor and
   baseline optical/matter-wave response. Reconcile paper/code absorption
   cross-section conventions before using an absolute quantum baseline.
B. Determine or explicitly condition on the scalar mass, occupation,
   temperature/nonequilibrium spectrum, and source preparation. Record a
   zero-population branch; coupling alone does not force a laboratory signal.
C. Compute finite-time moving-object harmonic filters in the applicable regime,
   including response, switching, renormalization, and mass/velocity averaging.
D. Obtain repeated blank/reference scans and detector calibration sufficient
   to specify count dispersion and temporal correlations. A negative-binomial,
   latent-rate or correlated model requires validation, not just a choice of
   a convenient variance inflation factor.
E. Choose independently measured interventions on at least two signal-relevant
   settings (e.g. velocity and geometry) whose predicted change cannot be
   reproduced by the calibrated instrument nuisance space. Preserve identical
   control acquisition and blind analysis labels where feasible.
F. Freeze predictions, nuisance priors/constraints, fit range and test statistic
   before evaluating a held-out acquisition. Demonstrate confidence-interval
   coverage with the validated measurement model and independent replication.

These deliverables define a measurement program with falsifiable predictions.
They do not create GATE03 device data, certify an unprovided apparatus, or
reinterpret the public authors' collapse-model result as a portal-field limit.
