MOMENTUM-RESOLVED FREE-BATH EVOLUTION
AND A CONDITIONAL ZERO-GAP THEOREM
Research continuation, 2026-10-21

1. Outcome and scope
--------------------
This continuation evolves two scalar distributions on a radial energy grid
in the same ideal bath used for the earlier moment calculation. It compares
specified population and energy perturbations with the original and enlarged
polynomial scalar spaces. The calculation preserves number, detailed balance,
and positivity, and reports separate quadrature, grid, and tail sensitivities.

At one reference time unit, the resolved population excess retains about
5.59% of its initial value, while the temperature-shaped energy excess retains
about 41.49%. At five units those residuals are about 0.03259% and 1.9896%.
The enlarged moment approximation describes these finite-time observables
more usefully than the count-only restriction, although some differences
between the enlarged moments and grid remain comparable to grid sensitivity.

A separate analytic result sharpens the global spectral question: if this
vacuum-tree scattering operator is extended unchanged to arbitrarily high
momentum, its uniform equilibrium-weighted L2 gap is exactly zero. That
conditional theorem does not preclude useful bulk relaxation. It changes
the appropriate numerical target from a positive global-gap certificate to
controlled evolution of specified distributions and observables.

This is original AI-assisted computation and analysis within a hypothetical
model. Internal independent-method checks were performed; external scientific
peer review and empirical validation are not claimed.

2. Fixed model and the meaning of time
--------------------------------------
The singlet masses are m1=10 and m2=11.5 GeV, the mediator mass is mh=125 GeV,
and the portal couplings are K11=K22=+0.001 and K12=-0.001. Only their squares
enter the included channels. The bath contains ideal Maxwell-Boltzmann e, mu,
tau and free u,d,s,c,b fermions, with quark masses 0.002, 0.005, 0.100, 1.300,
4.200 GeV. Spin and charge give a factor 4 per flavor; quarks additionally
carry color multiplicity 3. The exact vacuum-tree spacelike amplitude is

  |M_ijf(t)|^2 = Kij^2 mf^2 (4mf^2-t)/(mh^2-t)^2,  t<=0.

The temperature is held at T=0.4 GeV. Define the reference clock

  H_ref = sqrt(8*pi^3*60/90)*T^2/(1.2209e19 GeV)
        = 1.685248937226985e-19 GeV,
  tau = H_ref*t.

Here H_ref only provides a time unit. No Hubble redshift, cooling bath,
entropy evolution, or moving equilibrium is integrated. Number-changing
reactions, scalar self-collisions, decay redistribution, and interacting
QCD response are absent. The result is therefore a fixed-temperature
scattering benchmark, not an expanding-universe solution or relic abundance.

The dilute test-particle scattering equation is linear in the singlet
distribution when the bath is fixed. Thus the numerical semigroup evolves
actual positive cell populations, rather than only a list of moment rates.

3. Radial reversible discretization
------------------------------------
Use x_i=(E_i-m_i)/T. A cell specifies a species and an interval in x_i.
Its equilibrium mass pi_a retains the correct relative species abundance;
G=diag(pi_a). For an equilibrium scattering event of weight w joining
cells a and b, assemble

  D += w (e_a-e_b)(e_a-e_b)^T.

The event weights include the inherited elastic factor 1/2 and the paired
conversion contribution once. Equivalently, an oriented event histogram
E gives W=E+E^T and D=diag(W*1)-W. Self-cell events cancel. This construction
makes D symmetric and positive semidefinite, with D*1=0 and nonpositive
off-diagonals.

Relative occupations g=f/f_eq and cell masses m=Gg satisfy

  dg/dtau = -G^(-1)Dg/H_ref,
  dm/dtau = -D G^(-1)m/H_ref.

The mass generator has nonnegative off-diagonals and zero column sums.
Consequently its exact finite-dimensional semigroup preserves positivity
and total number, with pi stationary.

The finest event quadrature uses orders (q, boost, scattering, direction, azimuth)
=(80,64,40,40,48). Its raw graph contains 192 finite cells per species, of
width 0.125 through x=24, and one semi-infinite tail cell. The presented graph
aggregates this to 128 finite cells through x=16 plus a semi-infinite cell,
giving 258 states. Aggregation uses P^T D P and the corresponding aggregated
metric. All farther cells remain inside the tail; none is discarded or
turned into a reflecting boundary. Its equilibrium tail mass is 9.4504e-7.
The tail is retained as a lumped state, not resolved at arbitrarily high x.

4. Initial states and observables
---------------------------------
The equilibrium species fractions are approximately

  (r1,r2)=(0.972071506680683,0.027928493319317),

and the equilibrium mean total energy is 10.670619529578211 GeV. Two
mean-zero perturbations are initialized:

  h_P(i,E)=1_(i=2)-r2,
  h_E(i,E)=(E-<E>_pi)/T,
  f(0)=f_eq*(1+0.05*h).

The first changes the heavy-species population while preserving total
number. The second follows the normalized thermal distribution's energy
tangent. Its explicit linear-in-h definition is used; it is not an exact
thermal distribution at temperature 1.05T. Both specified initial states
are nonnegative. Cell values are equilibrium conditional averages of h.

The reported normalized autocorrelation is

  C_h(tau)=<h(0),h(tau)>_pi/<h(0),h(0)>_pi.

For h_P it equals the remaining heavy-species excess divided by its initial
excess. For h_E it equals the remaining mean-total-energy excess divided by
its initial excess. These are fractions of the initial perturbation, not
fractions of the entire particle population or total equilibrium energy.
The output also includes the squared L2 perturbation norm, species masses,
energy excess, and distribution snapshots.

Normalization needs care. The population shape is represented exactly on
every species grid. The energy shape loses within-cell variance on projection:

  continuum variance = 2.020726250162852,
  cell-projected variance = 2.0194309046730576,
  retained fraction = 0.9993589703257976.

Thus the presented grid retains 99.935897% of the continuum initial energy
variance, a projection loss of 0.064103%. Own-initial normalization sets
C_E(0)=1 on each discretization. A common-continuum normalization instead
divides every numerator by the continuum variance; the radial curve then
starts at 0.9993589703 and equals that factor times its own-normalized curve.
Both conventions are stored. For example, at tau=1 the radial energy value
is 0.414928883 with its own initial denominator and 0.414662901 with the
continuum denominator. This difference must not be attributed to dynamics.

6. Verification and empirical sensitivities
-------------------------------------------
The final graph reproduces the count-only contrast at 2.97474576498998 H_ref.
Its number-null relative matrix residual is 3.28e-16; the maximum recorded
number error is 4.44e-16. Both initial distributions remain positive over
the output interval. Their smallest recorded relative occupations,
including initialization, are about 0.99860 and 0.91987.

An independent calculation forms the forward mass generator and evaluates
its matrix exponential at tau=1. The transition matrix is nonnegative,
its maximum column-sum defect is 3.11e-15, and its stationary-mass error is
9.02e-17. Evolved cell masses agree with the whitened spectral method to
better than 1.05e-16. This tests generator orientation and evolution
separately from collision-integral accuracy.

Indicator cells introduce discontinuities into the event integrals.
The earlier polynomial-moment quadrature precision cannot simply be
assigned to this grid. Completed refinement comparisons give:

  - Final quadrature 64-to-80 changes, over the five reported times:
    at most 0.229% for population and 0.0742% for energy correlations.
  - Grid 64-to-128 changes on the finest event quadrature:
    at most 1.117% for population and 1.101% for energy, reached at tau=5.
    At tau=1 the corresponding changes are 0.1775% and 0.2163%.
  - Moving the lumped-tail start from x=16 to 24 changes these correlations
    by less than 3.24e-7 relatively over the same times.

These are observed refinement sensitivities, not confidence intervals,
certified error bounds, physical uncertainties, or a full continuum-
convergence proof. The final grid comparison is the largest listed
late-time sensitivity. Conservation and positivity are structural
properties and do not remove that resolution limitation.

7. Conditional zero-gap theorem
--------------------------------
For the hypothetical all-momentum extension of the same massive-propagator
scattering operator, let u=-t>=0. The amplitude has finite maximum

  A_ijf=Kij^2*mf^2/[4*(mh^2-4*mf^2)]

for the included masses, since mh^2>=8mf^2. Let a,b be incoming and outgoing
center-of-momentum magnitudes. From lambda_i=4s*a^2, t-interval width 4ab,
and v_M=sqrt(s)*a/(E_i*epsilon_f), the exact cross-section normalization gives

  v_M*sigma_ijf <= A_ijf*b/(16*pi*sqrt(s)*E_i*epsilon_f)
                <= A_ijf/(32*pi*E_i*epsilon_f).

The apparent exothermic threshold divergence in sigma cancels in the
product with relative velocity. The bound is uniform in the bath angle.
Integrating and summing the finite MB bath gives

  nu_i(p) <= B_i(T)/E_i(p),
  B_i(T)=sum_(f,j) [4*Nc*A_ijf/(32*pi)]
          * [mf*T*K1(mf/T)/(2*pi^2)].

B_i has units GeV^2. At T=0.4 GeV, both initial species have
B_i=6.630350823059819e-15 GeV^2, or B_i/H_ref=39343.45055259205 GeV.
This loose bound is not a predicted collision rate or a physical
decoupling threshold.

Normalize pi_i(dp) proportional to exp(-E_i/T)d^3p/(2*pi)^3 and consider
D_R={both species with E_i>=R}. For the centered radial indicator
h_R=1_(D_R)-pi(D_R), detailed balance makes its Dirichlet energy equal
to outward equilibrium flux. Therefore

  E(h_R,h_R)/||h_R||^2
    <= B_max/[R*(1-pi(D_R))] -> 0.

The indicators belong to the form domain because nu_i<=B_i/m_i is bounded.
Nonnegativity then proves the uniform L2 variational gap, after removing
the common-number direction, is exactly zero. The indicators are already
isotropic, so the obstruction exists in the radial sector.

The finite graph's smallest positive rate, approximately 0.73910 H_ref,
is compatible with this theorem. A finite grid and one lumped tail do
not represent indicators arbitrarily far into the tail. Consequently
that finite-graph rate is neither the continuum gap nor a positive lower
bound on it.

The theorem assumes the displayed tree amplitude unchanged at every
momentum, a fixed ideal MB bath, the stated scattering-only channel list,
an unbounded momentum domain, and the equilibrium-weighted L2 norm.
It does not establish a property of a physical ultraviolet completion,
interacting QCD, additional channels, or a cooling cosmological background.
It rules out one uniform exponential relaxation claim over all allowed
perturbations; it does not rule out useful relaxation of the bulk initial
states evolved here. The full proof and constants script are in tail/.

8. What remains to obtain physical evolution
---------------------------------------------
This calculation supplies a momentum-resolved benchmark and explicit
finite-time tests of moment truncation. Further numerical refinement can
target the remaining grid sensitivity and other specified initial shapes.
Physical cosmological evolution additionally requires a matched thermal
response, a complete reaction network including number-changing processes
and relevant decays, consistent thermodynamics and entropy history, and
declared initial conditions. The expanding equation must include redshift
and the changing bath. Apparatus calibration belongs to its separate
measurement program and is not inferred from these scattering trajectories.
