CONDITIONAL ZERO-GAP THEOREM
For the globally extended vacuum-tree, free-bath scattering-only model
2026-10-21

Status and scope
----------------
This note proves a mathematical property of the hypothetical operator
obtained by using the declared massive-propagator tree amplitude at every
momentum, with a fixed ideal Maxwell-Boltzmann bath and only the included
2-to-2 scalar-fermion scattering channels.

Under those explicit assumptions, its uniform L2 equilibrium spectral gap,
defined after removing the common-number direction, is exactly zero.
The same conclusion already holds in the isotropic radial sector.

This is not a claim that the physical theory is valid at arbitrarily high
energy, that QCD has zero gap, that a particular cosmological preparation
cannot thermalize, or that finite-time bulk relaxation is absent.

1. Model and collision frequency
--------------------------------
Let the test species i have energy E_i=sqrt(|p|^2+m_i^2), and let a bath
fermion f have energy epsilon=sqrt(|k|^2+m_f^2). For the channel

  s_i + f -> s_j + f,

use the initial-spin-averaged matrix element

  |M_ijf(t)|^2 = K_ij^2*m_f^2*(4*m_f^2-t)/(m_h^2-t)^2,  t<=0.

The exact spacelike property follows from t=(k-k')^2 and the equal
positive incoming/outgoing bath masses. The massive propagator is
retained; a contact approximation is not used.

The bath-frame collision frequency is

  nu_i(p) = sum_(f,j) g_f * integral d^3k/(2*pi)^3
            exp(-epsilon/T) * v_M(p,k) * sigma_ijf(s),

where g_f=4*N_c includes two spin states, both charges, and the quark
color multiplicity N_c. The final-species sum contains the elastic and
conversion channels from the fixed initial species. Closed channels
contribute zero.

In the center-of-momentum frame let a and b be the incoming and outgoing
three-momentum magnitudes. Then

  lambda_i(s) = 4*s*a^2,
  v_M = sqrt(lambda_i)/(2*E_i*epsilon)
      = sqrt(s)*a/(E_i*epsilon),
  sigma_ijf = integral_(t_minus)^(t_plus) |M_ijf(t)|^2 dt
              / (16*pi*lambda_i),
  t_plus-t_minus = 4*a*b.

These conventions agree with cross_section(), reduced_kernel(), and
t_limits() in the inherited leptonic_conversion_scattering.py.

2. A uniform bound on the amplitude
-----------------------------------
Put u=-t>=0, A=4*m_f^2, and B=m_h^2. Direct differentiation gives

  d/du [(A+u)/(B+u)^2] = (B-2*A-u)/(B+u)^3.

Consequently a global amplitude bound is

  Mmax_ijf =
    K_ij^2*m_f^2/[4*(m_h^2-4*m_f^2)], if m_h^2>=8*m_f^2;
    4*K_ij^2*m_f^4/m_h^4,           if m_h^2<8*m_f^2.

Here Mmax denotes the maximum of |M|^2, not of |M|.
The two expressions agree at equality. All included bath species in the
benchmark satisfy the first branch.

3. Collision-frequency bound
----------------------------
The amplitude bound and t-interval width imply

  v_M*sigma_ijf
    <= [sqrt(s)*a/(E_i*epsilon)]
       * [4*a*b*Mmax_ijf/(16*pi*4*s*a^2)]
     = Mmax_ijf*b/(16*pi*sqrt(s)*E_i*epsilon)
    <= Mmax_ijf/(32*pi*E_i*epsilon).

The last step uses b<=sqrt(s)/2, valid for nonnegative final masses.
The calculation is first made away from threshold. At an exothermic
initial threshold sigma can diverge as 1/a, but its product with v_M
has the displayed finite bound, so the inequality extends by a limit.
It is independent of the angle between p and k.

Therefore

  0 <= nu_i(p) <= B_i(T)/E_i(p),

where

  B_i(T) = sum_(f,j) g_f*Mmax_ijf/(32*pi) * I_f(T),

  I_f(T) = integral d^3k/(2*pi)^3 exp(-epsilon/T)/epsilon
         = m_f*T*K_1(m_f/T)/(2*pi^2).

The massless limiting integral is T^2/(2*pi^2), while this particular
Yukawa amplitude vanishes for m_f=0. B_i has units GeV^2; nu_i has units
GeV in natural units. The finite list of included channels gives finite
B_i for every fixed finite positive temperature.

It follows uniformly over species and directions that

  sup_(i,p:E_i(p)>=R) nu_i(p) <= B_max(T)/R -> 0.

For the inherited benchmark at T=0.4 GeV, with both final scalar species
and the three lepton plus five quark flavors, the bound gives

  B_1=B_2=6.630350823059819e-15 GeV^2,
  H=1.685248937226985e-19 GeV,
  B_i/H=39343.45055259205 GeV.

This is a deliberately loose global bound. It does not claim that the
actual collision rate equals B_i/E_i, or identify a physical decoupling
energy. The smallness argument ultimately uses arbitrarily remote
thermal tails, not a newly observed discrepancy at thermal momenta.

4. Tail test functions and the zero-gap conclusion
-------------------------------------------------
Normalize the stationary two-species measure as

  pi_i(dp) = Z^(-1)*exp(-E_i(p)/T)*d^3p/(2*pi)^3.

The common fugacity cancels. Microscopic reversibility and the fixed
MB bath give detailed balance for the scattering kernel k:

  pi_i(dp) k_ij(p,dp') = pi_j(dp') k_ji(p',dp).

Use the nonnegative collision Dirichlet form

  E(g,g) = (1/2) sum_(i,j) integral pi_i(dp) k_ij(p,dp')
           [g_i(p)-g_j(p')]^2.

Let D_R consist of both species with E_i(p)>=R, put p_R=pi(D_R), and
define the mean-zero isotropic function g_R=1_(D_R)-p_R. For every finite
R above the masses, 0<p_R<1. Its variance is

  ||g_R||_(L2(pi))^2 = p_R*(1-p_R).

The Dirichlet energy is the one-way equilibrium flux across the cut:

  E(g_R,g_R) = Q(D_R,D_R^c)
             <= integral_(D_R) pi(dx) nu(x)
             <= [B_max(T)/R]*p_R.

The factor 1/2 in the Dirichlet form cancels the two equal directed
cross-boundary fluxes. Thus

  E(g_R,g_R)/||g_R||^2
    <= B_max(T)/[R*(1-p_R)] -> 0,

because the normalized MB measure has p_R->0 as R->infinity.

The test functions belong to the form domain: the frequency bound also
gives a finite global bound nu_i<=B_i/m_i for these positive test-particle
masses. No smoothness of the indicator is required for this finite-rate
jump form.

Since the Dirichlet form is nonnegative, the variational gap satisfies

  lambda_* = inf_(g perpendicular to 1, g!=0) E(g,g)/||g||^2 = 0.

Every g_R is isotropic. Therefore this obstruction is already present
within the scalar radial sector; adding drift or quadrupole modes is
not needed for the proof.

5. Interpretation for numerical work
------------------------------------
A finite momentum grid or finite polynomial basis can have a positive
smallest nonzero eigenvalue. Such values remain useful diagnostics of
the retained distributions, but cannot converge to a positive uniform
gap of the globally extended operator proved above.

The zero gap rules out a positive uniform L2 exponential contraction
rate covering every mean-zero distribution perturbation. It does not
rule out useful finite-time relaxation estimates for specified initial
states, rapid relaxation of bulk observables, convergence without a
uniform exponential rate, or stronger estimates in a restricted class.

For the current calculation, appropriate numerical targets are
convergence of specified population/energy observables and distribution
distances over declared time intervals, together with momentum-grid,
quadrature, and tail sensitivity. A positive global-gap certificate is
not the appropriate target for this all-momentum scattering-only model.

6. Conditions that must not be silently changed
----------------------------------------------
The proof assumes:
  - the displayed massive-propagator amplitude at every momentum;
  - a fixed finite-temperature ideal MB bath and finite flavor list;
  - the declared finite-rate 2-to-2 scattering-only operator;
  - the equilibrium-weighted L2 norm and common-number projection;
  - an unbounded momentum domain.

It does not extend automatically to added interactions, a different
ultraviolet completion, a time-dependent expanding background, a
momentum cutoff, quantum/medium response, or a different functional norm.
