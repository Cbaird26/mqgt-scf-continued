DEPENDENCIES AMONG THE TEN SUPPLIED NEXT STEPS
2026-10-21 source audit and research planning

These are proposed completion criteria. No item is promoted on the basis
of this document. The pasted Grok list is treated as a work list, not
evidence of completed physics.

A. Apparatus chain: items1,2,8,9
-------------------------------
These items overlap. They must be linked in one auditable measurement
chain:

  calibrated source -> field at detector -> device response
  -> nuisance likelihood -> accessible bounds and exposure budget.

The five source objects in the combined collection, physical pp6681-6685:

1. Physical source J(x): calibrated measurable quantity, units, spatial
   and temporal support, geometry, boundaries, and uncertainty.
2. Field transfer and estimand: the actual E(x,t) at the detector and the
   parameter combination to which the observable responds.
3. Device generator/readout: actual apparatus operators, dynamics, and
   observed waiting times, rates, linewidths, or visibility, as appropriate.
4. Nuisance likelihood and controls: modeled confounds, instrument
   behavior, selection, serial correlation, and the specified nulls.
5. Accessible interval and sensitivity: numerical reach derived from
   actual noise, calibration, exposure, and the declared observable.

Item2 asks what laboratory quantity sources the model. Source density,
geometry, time dependence, boundaries, and a justified conversion to
J are missing physical inputs. A conventional actuator is not
automatically the hidden S field. A symbolic relation J=kappa_E*S^2
does not supply a calibration.

Item8 requires the normalized source, causal field solution at the
detector, and the device coupling law. Symbolic field work can proceed;
an apparatus-backed transfer function needs the physical records above.

Item9 requires detector response, acquisition specifications, noise,
live time, and controls before a defensible numerical integration time
or sensitivity can be assigned. The existing bound is not an accessible
signal target.

GATE-03 v0.2 is not an operator-ready protocol. Its current stub uses
prospective vocabulary involving live time and a statistic y-hat,
while leaving estimator, sampling plan, thresholds, and promotion rules
undefined. P(1) approximately1/2 remains a null control. The historical
v0.1 bit endpoint retains its original meaning. A visibility endpoint
from a separate packet must not silently become a second primary
endpoint for this rate-based apparatus proposal.

B. Thermal bath and cosmology: items4,5,7,6
------------------------------------------
Items4 and5 belong in one matched finite-temperature response problem.
Specify the renormalized operator basis, matching scale, coefficients,
thermal spectral functions, and uncertainty conventions.

If the scalar source is O_S=sum_a C_a O_a, its response is built from
the matrix of operator correlators, including cross terms where present.
An interacting quark/gluon response cannot generally be obtained by
adding unrelated free-particle rates. Heavy quarks counted explicitly
must not also be counted again through matched effective gluon terms.

The physically relevant response depends on energy transfer, momentum
transfer, and temperature. Vacuum timelike widths do not by themselves
supply the full thermal scattering response. Gluonic contributions and
the thermal/confinement treatment need defensible input, not an arbitrary
enhancement chosen to force an equilibration outcome.

Item7 specifies the full reaction network and thermodynamics: which
number-changing reactions, inverse reactions, decays and momentum
redistribution are present, with expansion/entropy/equation-of-state
inputs that are consistent with the chosen medium.

Item6 is full momentum-dependent evolution. A declared free-bath
benchmark can proceed now; a physical abundance prediction additionally
depends on items4,5,7 and initial conditions. Acceptance requires
documented momentum/time/tail convergence for the observables claimed,
and propagation of physical input uncertainty.

The present package advances item10 by a nested finite-space calculation.
It does not complete item6 or remove all finite-moment limitations.

C. Electromagnetic construction and coupling: item3
---------------------------------------------------
The October12 notes, collection physical pp7585-7598, already calculate
conditional normalized one-loop and shared spectral-action coefficients.
The next task therefore concerns what physically fixes the matching
data, normalization, and profile, and how the resulting coupling is
related to the measurement scale.

The one-loop kinetic term needs renormalization and finite matching
information. A finite higher-order field-strength coefficient does not
on its own determine the quadratic kinetic normalization.

The shared spectral-action relation is conditional on its overall
normalization and spectral profile. The supplied notes construct two
positive profiles with the same selected leading moments through a6
but different resulting couplings. Those leading constraints therefore
do not uniquely fix the coupling.

Completion requires a justified physical profile/normalization or other
selection principle, complete matching and electroweak embedding, and
evolution to the scale at which the coupling is compared with data.
A numerical fit alone is not a parameter-free prediction.

D. What is ready and what needs input
------------------------------------
Ready for further work now:
  - momentum-resolved and tail-sensitive free-bath benchmarks;
  - matched-response theory and uncertainty organization;
  - electromagnetic normalization/matching analysis;
  - apparatus source reconciliation and symbolic field calculations.

Actual missing inputs:
  - named source/detector and calibration/geometry/acquisition records;
  - defensible interacting finite-temperature response and matching;
  - completed reaction network, thermodynamics, and initial conditions;
  - physical normalization/profile selection for a predictive EM coupling.

These are specific scientific requirements, not a reason to stop every
branch of the research program.
