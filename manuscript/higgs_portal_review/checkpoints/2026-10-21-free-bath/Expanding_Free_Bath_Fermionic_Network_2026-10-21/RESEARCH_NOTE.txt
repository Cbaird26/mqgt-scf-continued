EXPANDING FREE-BATH EVOLUTION WITH THE FERMIONIC REACTION NETWORK
Research continuation — 21 October 2026

1. Result and interpretation

The momentum-resolved continuation has been carried through cosmological
redshift, scattering/conversion, three-body decay/inverse absorption, and
pair annihilation/inverse creation. Starting from a specified equilibrium
preparation at T=0.5 GeV and evolving to T=1/3 GeV, the scalar population
becomes colder than the bath and retains an excess heavy-species fraction.
The included number-changing reactions remove only 31.54 parts per million
of the initial comoving scalar number. These are results for a declared
leading-portal-order fermionic free-bath benchmark, not a physical QCD
calculation or a relic-abundance prediction.

The final, refined run gives:

Here Tkin=P/n=<p^2/(3E)> over both scalar species. This definition equals
the bath temperature for a classical Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution at
that temperature, including relativistic corrections. It is not identified
with two-thirds of the mean kinetic energy. The final mean kinetic energy is 0.386818 GeV,
and Tkin is approximately 0.250090 GeV.

The final heavy fraction is 3.6632%, whereas the instantaneous equilibrium
fraction is 1.3410%. TV common is half the sum of absolute differences
between normalized cell populations and a common-fugacity equilibrium
shape. TV shape uses equilibrium momentum shapes with each species'
actual abundance. Thus the latter isolates within-species shape departure.
Both are distances between projected distributions; neither is a fitted
temperature or a fraction of particles individually classified as thermal.

2. Initial state, normalization, and expansion clock

The inherited model has m1=10 GeV, m2=11.5 GeV, mh=125 GeV,
K11=K22=+0.001, and K12=-0.001 [1,2]. The interaction convention is

  Lint = -(v/2) h sum_ij Kij phi_i phi_j -(mf/v) h fbar f,
  vertex(h phi_i phi_j) = -i v Kij.

The bath contains e, mu, tau, u, d, s, c, and b, with inherited fixed
fermion masses, color multiplicities, and Maxwell–Boltzmann occupations.
Quantum statistical factors and medium-dependent masses are absent.
Both radiation and entropy degrees of freedom are held at g*=g*s=60.

The newly specified scalar initial condition is fi(p,T0)=exp[-Ei(p)/T0]
at T0=0.5 GeV: both chemical potentials vanish and the common fugacity is
one. This fixes the total physical density to 1.7005907436e-9 GeV^3 and
Y0=n0/s0=5.1691760109e-10. Earlier normalized scattering diagnostics did
not require an absolute abundance; annihilation does. This preparation
does not show that earlier evolution established chemical equilibrium.

With a0=1, entropy conservation in the prescribed background gives
a=T0/T. The coordinates y=p/T and eta=ln(T0/T) remove the free-streaming
advection. The interval ends at eta=ln(1.5)=0.405465. For the specified
radiation Hubble law, its physical duration is H(T0) Delta t=0.625, or
H(0.4 GeV) Delta t=0.4. This differs from the previous fixed-temperature
relaxation clock. The final physical density is 5.0386284863e-10 GeV^3;
its large decrease principally reflects dilution. The final yield is
5.1690129969e-10, a fractional decrease of 3.15358e-5.

3. Included reactions and normalization

The evolved channels are phi_i f <-> phi_j f and their charge conjugates;
phi_i phi_j <-> f fbar for pairs 11,12,22; and phi2 <-> phi1 f fbar.
The last channel is open for e,mu,u,d,s. Scattering and decay preserve
one scalar per transition. Only pair annihilation/inverse production
changes total scalar number in this network.

Crossing the inherited scattering amplitude requires removing its initial
fermion spin average. For annihilation the spin/color-summed expression is

  |M|^2 = 2 Nc Kij^2 mf^2 (s-4mf^2)/Dh(s),
  Dh(s) = (s-mh^2)^2+(mh Gamma_h)^2,
  sigma vM = Nc Kij^2 mf^2 (s-4mf^2)^(3/2)
             /[16 pi sqrt(s) Ei Ej Dh(s)].

The final spins and color are already summed; no bath spin/charge factor
four is added. An unordered identical-pair event rate has a factor one-half,
but each event removes two scalars. Consequently no extra one-half enters
the tagged-particle loss equation. These conventions are independently
checked by contraction against an invariant thermal integral.

Let Ma=a^3 na/n0 denote a comoving momentum-cell population, and let
Meq,a=a^3 neq,a(T)/n0. With Aab the positive symmetric cell average of
sigma vM, the pair term is

  dMa/deta = [n0/(a^3 H)] sum_b Aab [Meq,a Meq,b - Ma Mb].

The inverse source therefore uses absolute zero-chemical-potential
equilibrium populations. Replacing them by a unit-normalized shape would
give the wrong abundance. This expression vanishes at instantaneous
equilibrium and has a nonnegative source at an empty cell [3].

The free-fermion vacuum decay width is 1.1372679334e-21 GeV. Its e+mu
part, 3.0602676910e-22 GeV, reproduces the inherited leptonic result.
The quark contribution is a free-quark diagnostic, not a hadronic width.
The decay graph includes parent time dilation, the differential three-body
invariant-mass spectrum, and daughter recoil. Daughter-cell probabilities
use the exact isotropic rest-frame angular distribution after a boost.
A symmetric equilibrium conductance supplies inverse absorption once,
preserving scalar number and classical detailed balance.

For orientation, at T=(0.5,0.4,1/3) GeV the equilibrium annihilation loss
rate neq <sigma_eff v>/H is (1.012e-4,7.306e-7,5.273e-9). The linearized
common-fugacity chemical rate is twice this. The corresponding equilibrium
decay/inverse species-contrast rates are (0.00430,0.00660,0.00944) H.
These equilibrium diagnostics do not replace integration of the evolving
nonthermal distributions.

4. New timelike-width prescription and omitted channels

The crossed timelike propagator requires an additional prescription.
A zero-width pole is nonintegrable over unbounded thermal tails even
when the resonant population is exponentially small. This calculation
explicitly adopts Gamma_h=0.0041 GeV, a nominal Standard Model width
scale documented by the PDG [4]. No separate real-Higgs bath process is
added to the same inclusive annihilation kernel.

The independent thermal calculation bounds the entire omitted remote
energy region, including the resonance, using Dh>=(mh Gamma_h)^2 and
positive Bessel-function tail bounds. Its relative contribution is below
4.54e-24 in the declared temperature interval. This is an equilibrium
thermal-contraction bound for the specified prescription, not a uniform
bound on arbitrary nonthermal tails or on missing physical processes.
Halving/doubling the width changes bulk thermal rates by less than
8.69e-10/3.48e-9 relatively. Adding the approximately 1.9008e-5 GeV
portal scalar partial width shifts those rates by about 1.08e-11.

Known Higgs exchange also mediates scalar-only 2->2 channels at O(K^4)
in their cross sections. They were audited but are outside this evolved
O(K^2) fermionic network. At T0 their equilibrium tagged rates are
1.23766e-5 H and 3.22551e-5 H, with average 1.35139e-5 H. These are
equilibrium diagnostics, not trajectory-wide bounds. Independent scalar
quartic/cubic couplings are not numerically fixed by the inherited
scattering benchmark. Loop-induced gluon/photon channels, interacting
QCD response, confinement, and a physical hadronic decay spectrum also
remain uncalculated. Their absence limits the physical interpretation.

5. Momentum evolution and structural checks

The final discretization uses 192 finite y cells of width 0.25 through
y=48, plus one semi-infinite cell per species: 386 populations in total.
Collision conductances are tabulated on 16 uniform eta intervals with
the exact T=0.4 snapshot inserted, giving 18 temperature nodes. Positive
conductances are interpolated, while the instantaneous equilibrium cell
metric is recomputed. The linear scattering/decay generator has zero
column sums and annihilates that metric.

Evolution combines a positive pair-reaction update with a matrix
exponential for scattering/decay, using symmetric splitting and midpoint
coefficients. The nominal 192 eta steps become 195 after inserting
observation temperatures. No population clipping or number renormalization
is used. All populations remain positive; the smallest recorded comoving
cell population is 1.83e-14. The maximum linear-step number discrepancy is
3.33e-16. Scattering-only and scattering-plus-decay controls preserve
comoving number to 2.89e-15 across the run.

At T0 the conditional pair kernel gives <sigma_eff v>=
1.5675786424e-14 GeV^-2, agreeing with the independent invariant thermal
integral at floating-point precision. The decay graph reproduces its
independent time-dilated thermal contraction to better than 9e-14.
The T=0.4 inherited count contrast is recovered to 2.2e-12 relatively;
number-null and equilibrium-stationarity matrix residuals are roundoff.
These are normalization and structure tests, separate from convergence.

Collisionless cell populations remain exactly constant in y. Observable
reconstruction assumes the instantaneous equilibrium conditional shape
inside each cell, introducing a finite-cell closure error. An independent
continuum free-streaming integral gives final Tkin/T=0.7090601. The
192-cell reconstruction differs by 0.01273%; errors decrease by about
four on each doubling from 48 to 96 to 192 cells. Collisions raise the
final ratio to 0.7502713 while leaving substantial departure from the bath.

6. Channel controls and empirical numerical sensitivities

With scattering alone, the final heavy fraction is 0.03666502 and
Tkin/T=0.75027595. Adding decay/inverse absorption gives 0.03663220 and
0.75027112 while conserving number. Adding pair reactions gives
0.03663216 and 0.75027134, together with the 31.54 ppm number loss.
Thus the included pair network has a tiny bulk-shape effect for this
initial abundance and interval. Small channel differences are controlled
comparisons within the same discretization, not general accuracy bounds.

7. Consequences and provenance

The earlier fixed-bath relaxation and conditional zero uniform L2 gap
remain compatible. The new calculation addresses a different question:
competition between expansion and a specified reaction network over a
finite interval. The previous scattering-only gap theorem does not
automatically extend to this time-dependent nonlinear system. No new
global spectral-gap theorem is claimed here.

Further physical interpretation requires the missing medium response,
reaction channels, scalar-sector inputs, and thermal history. The saved
yield is conditional on the chosen initial preparation, not a predicted
late-time relic abundance. The background is prescribed; no self-consistent
entropy or radiation backreaction has been solved.

results/convergence_summary.json. network/NETWORK_AUDIT.txt is the reaction
